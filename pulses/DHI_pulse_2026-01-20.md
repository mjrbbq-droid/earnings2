## D.R. Horton (DHI) — Company Pulse (FY26 1Q, call 2026-01-20)

### Latest Quarter Take
Demand is holding *functionally steady*, but the *quality/price of demand* is weakening as affordability pressure forces DHI to “buy” orders with deeper incentives and rate buydowns. Management is explicitly guiding to a near-term margin step-down because incentive levels *exited the quarter higher* than the incentive levels embedded in 1Q closings/backlog.

- Demand tone: “New home demand remains impacted by affordability constraints and cautious consumer sentiment.” (Romanowski)
- Incentive stance: “We increased our sales incentives during the first quarter and we expect incentives to remain elevated in fiscal 2026.” (Romanowski)
- Near-term margin outlook driven by exit-rate incentives: “Exiting incentive levels in December are heavily coloring our margin outlook for the second quarter.” (Murray)

Directionally vs last 4 quarters (FY25 2Q–FY26 1Q): incentives/buydowns are **intensifying**, pricing power **deteriorating**, and gross margin **stepping down**, while cycle times and spec control are **improving**.

## Sales Pace & Demand Quality
**Sales pace appears stable but increasingly incentive-supported.** The company continues to cite affordability and sentiment as the binding constraint, implying traffic may exist but conversion is being “purchased” via financing concessions rather than true price strength.

- “We kept our lowest 30-year fixed rate offering at 3.99%.” (Hansen)
- Incentives are now described at a higher band than recent quarters: “We’ve been running at a high single-digit percentage. Today, we might have ticked up to low double-digit.” (Hansen)

**Cancellations are controlled (a demand-quality positive)** and improved sequentially:
- “Our cancellation rate for the quarter was 18%, consistent with the prior year quarter and down from 20% sequentially.” (Wheat)  
(Sequential improvement from FY25 4Q’s 20% supports that buyers are still qualifying/committing, even if the sale requires concessions.)

## Backlog & Backlog Quality
Backlog commentary is limited this quarter; instead, management leans on *incentive exit-rate* and *interest rate lock dynamics* to explain coming margin pressure—suggesting backlog is carrying higher incentive costs than what flowed through 1Q closings.

This is consistent with prior quarters’ pattern that “recent sales… in backlog do reflect the higher cost of incentives.” (Wheat, FY25 3Q), reinforcing that backlog mix is not a margin shelter right now.

## Spec Mix, Inventory & Capital Posture
**Specs are being deliberately reduced** because faster cycle times lower the need to carry finished homes. That is a genuine operational improvement and also a risk-control move given “pockets” of inventory pressure.

- “The spec count reduction has been deliberate and purposeful… because we can build homes faster than we ever have been able to.” (Murray)
- Cycle time improvement continues: “median cycle time… decreased two weeks from a year ago.” (Romanowski)

**Inventory is still sizable, but aged risk appears contained:**
- “We ended the quarter with 30,400 homes in inventory, of which 20,000 were unsold.” (Romanowski)
- “900 of our unsold homes have been completed for greater than six months.” (Romanowski)
- Market nuance: “we still see pockets of elevated inventory, and that is market-to-market.” (Romanowski)

Direction of change vs the last few quarters: total inventory and completed/aged risk metrics have **improved**, while management acknowledges localized oversupply (Florida noted in FY25 4Q; now generalized to “pockets”).

## Margins & Incentives Mechanics (be specific)
**1Q margin was flattered by a discrete item; underlying margin is already lower and 2Q is guided lower still due to higher incentive exit-rates.**
- “Excluding the 40 basis point benefit… our home sales gross margin would have been 20%.” (Hansen)
- “Incentives increased as we moved throughout the quarter so we expect our home sales gross margin to be lower in the second quarter.” (Hansen)
- 2Q guide: “We expect our home sales gross margin for the second quarter to be in the range of 19% to 19.5%.” (Hansen)

**Incentive composition is predominantly financing (rate buydowns), not broad sticker price cuts:**
- “Mostly incentives based on buy-downs… driving the Q2 levels.” (Wheat)
- Temporary buydowns are rising: “We did see a tick up in temporary buy-downs… more like a low double-digit percentage this quarter.” (Hansen)
- Management quantifies the incentive band: “high single-digit… ticked up to low double-digit.” (Hansen)

**Cost offsets are present but not enough to outrun incentives:**
- “Stick and brick costs were down roughly 1% and lot costs increased 2%.” (Hansen)
- And lot inflation persists: “We expect our lot costs to continue to be up sequentially and on a year-over-year basis.” (Hansen)

Mechanically, the bridge is clear: modest vertical-cost relief + cycle-time efficiencies are being overwhelmed by higher incentive/buydown costs flowing from late-quarter sales and locked-rate commitments.
