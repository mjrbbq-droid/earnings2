# KBH (KBH) — Company Pulse (FY25 Q4 focus)

## Demand, Sales Pace & Backlog Quality
**Latest quarter (FY25 Q4): demand looks *stable on traffic* but slower decision-making; conversion is a bright spot, yet backlog coverage is thinning.** Management repeatedly framed the issue as *time-to-decision* rather than outright demand collapse: “Traffic in our communities was steady during the fourth quarter” and “Consumers are demonstrating their interest… They’re just taking much longer to make their home-buying decisions.” Conversion appears to have improved at the margin: “Traffic’s held steady and our conversion is actually improved a little.”

**Backlog is the main deterioration signal.** They start FY26 with less embedded delivery coverage: “Although we begin the year with a lower backlog than we have carried in some time,” with backlog covering “27%… delivery target compared to 34% at the start of 2025.” Cancellations remain controlled and supportive of close certainty: “At 18%, our cancellation rate was stable,” and they emphasized buyer quality: “17%” down payment, “FICO score of 743,” and “10%… all cash buyers.”

**Direction vs prior 3 quarters (FY25 Q1–Q3):**
- Demand: Q1–Q2 softened (“more muted”; “more subdued”), then stabilized in Q3 (“stability in demand… continued”), and stayed stable in Q4 (steady traffic, slightly better conversion).
- Backlog: deteriorated all year as orders lagged and cycle times improved (“24% reduction in our ending backlog”), culminating in lower starting FY26 backlog coverage.

## Specs, Mix & Inventory Posture
**Spec exposure remains a margin overhang, but the mix strategy is explicitly shifting back to BTO.** Q4 deliveries were still spec-heavy: “returning our Built to Order homes… from 57% in Q4 2025 to… 70% or higher.” Management acknowledges near-term inertia: “In the first quarter… probably that 57% to 60% range.”

**Inventory is tangible and partially aged (cost-basis problem).** They disclosed “about 1,700 homes in inventory… a little over 1,000… at or near the finish stage,” and flagged that some needs clearing: “There is still some inventory that we have to clear… some of this inventory is aged… higher cost basis.” This sets up continued pricing/incentive pressure risk until the older specs burn off.

**Competitive/inventory backdrop:** competitive discounting is described as a spec-led phenomenon: “Builders are trying to cover their spec inventory… discounting… giving rate buy downs.” This matters because KBH is trying to avoid chasing that game while still carrying its own elevated spec inventory.

## Margins & Incentives Mechanics (be specific)
**FY25 Q4 margin declined sharply, and management was explicit on the drivers: price/land/mix.** “Adjusted housing gross profit margin was 310 basis points lower due to pricing pressure… higher relative land costs… mix.” That is a worse slope than earlier FY25 quarters (Q1/Q2 cited land/concessions/pricing with partial cost offsets), implying mix/spec and pricing became more acute into Q4.

**BTO vs spec is the core mechanical lever.** They quantified the spread: “Our gross margins on BTO homes are trending 3 to 5 percentage points higher than on inventory sales.” That spread explains why mix, not just costs, is now the dominant narrative for FY26 margin recovery: “We expect margins to improve throughout 2026… shift the mix… to favor Built to Order homes.”

**Incentives / rate buydowns: KBH claims restraint, but the market is incentive-heavy; KBH’s mortgage concession level appears to have come down materially vs earlier FY25.**
- Q4: mortgage concessions “right around 1%.”
- Earlier FY25 Q1: “Mortgage concessions represented approximately 2% of our housing revenues… two-thirds… rate buydowns.”
This is consistent with the company’s described strategy of limiting incentives and using pricing discipline: “We were disciplined in not taking overly aggressive steps to capture sales.” However, restraint can collide with the need to clear aged, higher-cost specs—creating near-term gross margin drag even if concession % stays low (because price cuts/closing mix can do the work).

**Near-term guide framing:** Q1 FY26 housing gross margin guided to **15.4%–16%** (“expected to be between 15.4% and 16%”) with pressure from “negative operating leverage… pricing pressure and higher lot costs.” The magnitude implies mix (spec clearing) and deleverage are expected to swamp the cost-down tailwind in the near term.

## Cost Relief vs Cost Pressure
**Cost relief is real and increasingly quantified—especially on new starts—yet it is arriving alongside worse land cost and mix.**
- Build/cycle time: “Build times improving roughly 20% year-over-year,” and “120 days or better… several divisions averaging fewer than 100 days in November.”
- Direct cost: “Direct costs… about 4% lower sequentially and 6% lower year-over-year on our homes started during the fourth quarter.”
- Unit cost: “average cost per unit declined… direct construction cost and material cost declined more than lot costs increased.”

**Cost pressure:** management keeps returning to “higher relative land costs” as a persistent headwind in the margin bridge. They also admitted the aged spec cost basis problem, which is not a “cost” line item but behaves like one via margin compression when cleared.

## Guidance Credibility & Visibility
**Tone is cautiously defensive: they reaffirm some items, but they also *withheld* key profitability guidance until next quarter.** They provided FY26 deliveries/revenue but deferred margin outlook: “We are providing our outlook for fiscal 2026 deliveries and housing revenues… provide… gross margins… when we report… first quarter results.” That choice reduces near-term visibility and suggests they want another quarter of evidence on spec clearing + BTO mix before pinning down full-year profitability.

**Credibility positives:** Q4 includes concrete operational metrics (cycle time, direct cost down, inventory counts, concession %), and they are willing to quantify the BTO margin premium (3–5 pts).  
**Credibility watch-outs:** the margin recovery plan leans heavily on a mix shift that they simultaneously admit will not show up immediately (“first quarter… 57% to 60%”), while starting backlog coverage is lower (“27%… compared to 34%”).
