## INDUSTRY PULSE — HOMEBUILDING (as of 2026-03-13; latest earnings calls only)

### (1) Regime framing: “Rate-sensitive demand, incentive-led clearing, margins in a controlled reset”
Across public builders, the demand signal is mostly **stable to softer**, with **pockets of improvement in traffic** but weaker **conversion** and/or **backlog erosion**. The dominant mechanism to hold absorptions is **mortgage-related incentives (buydowns/closing costs)**, which is pushing the group into a **margin-contraction regime** even where volumes are holding up. Operationally, **cycle times are improving** (a genuine positive), but the benefit is being recycled into **inventory management and pace protection** rather than clean price realization.

Dispersion is widening: some operators are leaning into **mix shifts (BTO vs spec), cost-down, and community growth** to protect forward earnings power; others are more exposed to **spec-heavy clearing, competitive geographies (notably TX pockets), and weaker backlog visibility**.

---

### (2) Bullish themes showing up on calls
- **Cycle-time / throughput improvement is real and broad-based** (DHI, LEN, PHM, TMHC, KBH, CCS, GRBK). Faster builds improve turns and allow tighter spec management.
- **Cost relief / value engineering is helping cushion the incentive hit** (LEN highlighted structural cost reductions; KBH/CCS cited direct cost/material deflation and simplification; TMHC emphasized overhead efficiencies).
- **Community count growth as a volume backstop** (DHI +12% YoY communities but moderating; LEN +18% YoY; TOL targeting +8–10%; KBH planning openings ramp; LGIH targeting 10–15% by end of 2026).
- **Balance sheets remain generally low-stress with ongoing buybacks** (DHI, LEN, PHM, TMHC, KBH, MTH, TPH all explicitly maintained active capital return postures).
- **Selective bright spots by segment**: PHM’s **active adult/Del Webb** held up better; TOL’s **affluent buyer** is less rate-sensitive; installers/distributors (BLD, IBP) cite **heavy commercial** as a stabilizer even as residential slows.

---

### (3) Bearish themes showing up on calls
- **Pricing power is fading; incentives are rising** as the clearing tool (DHI exited the quarter at higher incentives; PHM incentives up to 8.9% of gross; GRBK discounts/incentives up; HOV incentives 12.2% of ASP; SDHC margin guidance stepped down on higher incentives/buydowns).
- **Gross margin outlook skews lower near-term** for most builders (DHI guided Q2 margin down; LEN guided Q1 margin 15–16% and called it the low point; PHM lowered Q4 GM guide; TMHC guided Q4 GM down on spec mix; TPH guided down; SDHC guided down further).
- **Backlog is generally deteriorating**, reducing near-term visibility (PHM backlog down materially; TOL lower beginning backlog; KBH lower starting backlog; GRBK backlog value down 20% YoY; MTH backlog down as conversion runs very high).
- **Spec/quick move-in intensity is elevated** in multiple models, which supports closings but tends to worsen margin/price quality (PHM spec share ~50% vs 40–45% target; TMHC spec inventory elevated though reducing starts; MHO inventory homes 75% of sales; HOV QMI 73% of sales; BZH expects 1Q spec up to 75%).
- **Affordability/consumer confidence remains the binding constraint**, especially at entry-level (repeated by DHI/LEN/PHM/TMHC/KBH/CCS; IBP calls out entry-level softness; several mention longer decision cycles and “choppy” demand).

---

### (4) Winners / laggards (based on relative call strength + deep-signature evidence)

**Winners (best relative setup / improving forward narrative)**
- **KBH**: Stands out for a more constructive forward mix story—explicit push back toward **70%+ built-to-order** from a spec-heavy mix, combined with **cost deflation/value engineering** and an expectation that **margins expand through 2026** as mix normalizes. Also highlighted strong liquidity and a large buyback authorization.
- **BLD (Builders FirstSource)**: Relative winner within the broader “homebuilding complex” because management **raised EBITDA guidance** with **M&A + cost actions** offsetting residential softness, and cited **steady commercial/industrial** as a bright spot—even while acknowledging residential weakness.
- **LGIH**: Clearer positive demand inflection in its call—**orders/backlog improving**, plus explicit commentary on **improving affordability** (rate decline + promoted ARM). Margins still pressured by mix/interest, but the demand trajectory was comparatively better.
- **TPH / MHO (second tier)**: Not “clean” wins, but comparatively better tone on balance sheet flexibility and managing incentives/spec; both emphasize credit quality and liquidity while navigating a choppy market.

**Laggards (weaker demand/margin quality, higher clearing pressure)**
- **PHM**: One of the weakest combinations—**orders/backlog deteriorating, cancellations up**, incentives higher, and spec share running above target for “several quarters,” with competition noted as high (TX/West soft).
- **TOL**: Despite strong backlog quality and less rate sensitivity, the call framed a **choppy environment** with **lower backlog into FY26** and **gross margin stepping down** alongside higher incentives vs prior year.
- **SDHC**: Most challenged near-term margin profile among the group provided—gross margin down sharply with **further downside guided**, driven by **rising incentives and buydown costs** plus higher lot cost burden.
- **CVCO / SKY (manufactured housing)**: More cautious tone—regional slowdowns (CVCO Southeast), higher inventories at retail, and tariff/lumber duty risks; SKY expects a near-term margin downshift and notes channel moderation.
- **HOV / BZH**: Heavy reliance on incentives/spec clearing. HOV called out very high incentive load and limited visibility (only next-quarter guidance). BZH guided a weak start to FY26 including a **1Q net loss** and highlighted a difficult sales environment.

**Middle of the pack (stable demand, but margin reset still in motion)**
- **DHI / LEN / TMHC / CCS / GRBK**: Generally stable-to-choppy demand with improving operations and cost actions, but each is explicitly managing through **incentive-driven margin pressure** and some level of backlog/ASP headwind.

---

### (5) What to watch next quarter (key swing factors)
1. **Incentive “step-up” vs “plateau”**: Several builders indicated incentives increased exiting the quarter (e.g., DHI) or remain elevated (LEN ~14%; PHM rising; HOV very high). Watch whether incentives stabilize, or if spring selling requires another leg up.
2. **Backlog stabilization and quality of orders**: With backlog down across many, the key will be whether spring traffic converts without disproportionate discounting—and whether quick move-in mixes keep crowding out higher-margin BTO.
3. **Spec inventory trajectory**: Are companies truly reducing completed/unsold specs (DHI improved; TMHC pulled back starts; KBH stopped starting specs), or does inventory re-accumulate if absorptions soften?
4. **Margin “trough” timing**: LEN framed Q1 as a likely low point; HOV expects GM to bottom in 1Q26; others guided near-term step-downs. Next quarter will clarify whether this is a **contained trough** or a **longer grind**.
5. **Geographic stress points**: Repeated mentions of **Texas competitiveness/inventory** (PHM, TMHC, MHO, BZH) and selective softness elsewhere. Watch for market-level commentary on resale competition and incentives becoming more localized.
6. **Input costs and tariffs**: Multiple mentions of tariff-related cost risk (PHM flagged potential 2026 build-cost impact; CVCO estimates tariff COGS; SKY expects tariff headwind rising; LEGH cites ~$1,200/home tariff impact). Confirmation or escalation becomes a real 2H sensitivity.

Net: the group is operating in a **volume-protection, incentive-clearing regime** with **operational execution improving** but **economics compressing**. The next quarter is about whether spring demand allows **incentive discipline** and **backlog stabilization**, or whether the industry extends the margin reset to keep pace.
