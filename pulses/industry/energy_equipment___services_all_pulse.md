## INDUSTRY PULSE — Energy Equipment & Services (as of 2026-03-13; latest earnings calls only)

### 1) Regime framing: late-cycle bifurcation (offshore/intl strength vs US land + trade noise)
The group is operating in a **two-speed regime**. Offshore and longer-cycle international programs remain constructive (often with backlog/contract cover and mix-driven margin lift), while **US land is the pressure valve** (activity softness, pricing giveback in commoditized lines) and **trade/tariff uncertainty is an incremental shock** showing up in costs and demand caution. Dispersion is widening: companies tied to subsea/manufactured products and contracted offshore vessels sound more confident than those exposed to short-cycle, price-sensitive product lines.

---

### 2) Bullish themes showing up on calls
- **Offshore/subsea durability and backlog conversion**
  - **FTI** described a durable subsea cycle “through the decade,” strong orders/direct awards and continued >1 book-to-bill, with higher iEPCI/Subsea 2.0 mix supporting margin expansion.
  - **OIS** highlighted decade-high Offshore Manufactured Products backlog and >1 book-to-bill, with revenue mix now ~75% offshore/international (longer-cycle, typically higher margin).
  - **OII** pointed to higher-margin manufactured products (umbilicals) backlog conversion.

- **Tight supply / improving contract economics in offshore marine**
  - **TDW** emphasized fleet rollovers into higher day-rate contracts and limited OSV supply growth/orderbook, supporting a steadier pricing backdrop even if there’s near-term whitespace.

- **Self-help and cost actions cushioning the downcycle pockets**
  - **WFRD** is leaning on cost initiatives/headcount reductions to protect/expand margins despite pricing pressure.
  - **OIS** cited restructuring/optimization in US land intended to structurally raise margin profile and improve free cash flow characteristics.

- **Capital return / balance sheet flexibility as a differentiator**
  - **FTI** stressed net cash, increased buyback authorization, and a commitment to return at least 70% of free cash flow.
  - **OII** flagged strong cash generation and buybacks.
  - **TDW** and **WFRD** both discussed refinancing steps that improve flexibility and interest burden.

- **Adjacent growth vector: behind-the-meter power for data centers**
  - **SEI** is riding data-center/AI power demand, expanding its capacity plan materially by early 2028 and noting tightening OEM slots/lead times (a demand signal, even if it’s also a constraint).

---

### 3) Bearish themes showing up on calls
- **Pricing pressure and commoditization (especially Middle East / undifferentiated services)**
  - **WFRD** explicitly called out pricing pressure, particularly in commodity-like service lines, alongside macro headwinds.

- **Tariffs/trade uncertainty hitting costs, volumes, and margins**
  - **WFRD** cited tariff-driven cost increases and volume impacts in certain US product lines and warned trade uncertainty could drive demand destruction in the short-to-mid term.
  - **OIS** took a direct hit: tariff-driven input cost spikes (China-sourced downhole consumables) contributed to negative EBITDA in Downhole; working on pass-through and supply-chain changes.

- **US land weakness remains the main near-term drag**
  - **WFRD** expects softness and seasonality to drive weaker near-term comparisons; US land declining.
  - **TTI** noted continued weakness expected in US onshore (lower frac crew count).
  - **SEI** called Lower-48 oil & gas activity near-term trough impacting its logistics utilization.

- **Timing/seasonality and utilization volatility**
  - **FTI** flagged seasonal Q4 offshore vessel utilization impacts (notably North Sea).
  - **OII** guided to a Q4 EBITDA step-down on seasonality/timing.
  - **TDW** described a near-term drilling “shoulder period” limiting the ability to push day rates aggressively despite supportive longer-term setup.

- **Collections/working-capital risk in Mexico (pops up across names)**
  - **WFRD**: collections uncertainty and possible deferrals.
  - **TDW**: Mexico customer payments delayed (working-capital watch item).

### 4) Winners vs laggards (based on signal strength from calls + relative scoring dispersion)

**Relative winners (stronger call momentum / clearer operating leverage):**
- **FTI**: Most consistent “quality-growth” read-through—raised EBITDA and free cash flow guidance, positive subsea mix narrative, high visibility, low competitive pressure, and explicit shareholder return framework.
- **WFRD**: Despite pricing headwinds, the call still read as improving execution—raised outlook, cost actions, and balance sheet moves; however, the narrative is more mixed due to pricing/tariffs.
- **TTI**: Raised EBITDA guide and positioned for offshore/international growth (Brazil/North Sea, Argentina wins) while managing US onshore weakness via automation and cost control.

**Middle of the pack (constructive but with notable offsets):**
- **SEI**: Strong structural demand driver (data centers/AI) and improving financial flexibility, but near-term quarterly EBITDA can be mix/utilization sensitive; also not a pure-play oilfield upcycle signal.
- **TDW**: Supportive medium-term setup (limited supply, higher day-rate rollovers, decent 2026 cover), but near-term whitespace/regional pressure and working-capital watch items temper momentum.

**Relative laggards (more pronounced headwinds / lower confidence tone):**
- **OII**: Operationally executing (strong 3Q) but the setup looks more timing-sensitive with an expected Q4 step-down and 2026 OPG uncertainty tied to customer schedules; utilization not fully tight.
- **OIS**: Offshore manufactured products looks strong, but Downhole is being squeezed by tariffs + softer activity; the story hinges on pass-through/supply-chain fixes and continued offshore mix shift.
- **SND**: Dated call relative to the rest of the group and described sequential declines in volumes/price; the forward view leaned on a 2025 pickup and possible pricing improvement rather than visible near-term traction.

---

### 5) What to watch next quarter (key swing factors)
- **Evidence of tariff cost recovery**: pass-through success, supplier re-sourcing, and margin stabilization—particularly for **WFRD** (US product lines) and **OIS** (Downhole consumables).
- **US land trajectory**: whether frac/Lower-48 activity stops deteriorating (impacts **WFRD, TTI, OIS, SEI** logistics).
- **Offshore backlog conversion and mix**: confirmation that subsea/manufactured products backlogs translate to revenue and incremental margins (**FTI, OIS, OII**), and whether book-to-bill stays supportive.
- **Marine utilization/day-rate realization vs whitespace**: whether **TDW** can keep utilization and pricing stable through the shoulder period and regional softness.
- **Mexico receivables/collections normalization**: cadence and disclosures from **WFRD** and **TDW**.
- **Guidance posture into 2026**: any shift from “back-half weighted” assumptions to more balanced visibility, especially where customers are pushing schedules “to the right” (notably **OIS/OII** commentary).

Overall, the industry’s current regime is **not a uniform upcycle**; it’s a **selective offshore/long-cycle expansion layered over short-cycle friction (US land + trade)**—and earnings outcomes are increasingly determined by mix, contract coverage, and the ability to defend margins against tariff-driven cost inflation.
