## IBP (Installed Building Products) — Company Pulse (FY25 3Q focus)

### Sales Pace & Demand Quality (Latest Q: FY25 3Q)
Residential demand looks **stable-to-soft on volume but supported by mix/price discipline**. Same-branch single-family installation sales were down despite decent price/mix, implying **lower unit/job throughput rather than price collapse**: “**Same branch new single-family installation sales were down 2%**” (Edwards). Consolidated operating cadence shows **price/mix doing the work while volumes fade**: “**Price/mix… 1.5% increase… offset by a 4.8% decrease in job volumes**” (Miller).

**Demand quality is improving in mix terms** (tilt toward higher-value customers/regions), even as affordability remains a cap on entry-level elasticity: “**Homeownership remains incredibly expensive… will remain the biggest challenge for our customers selling new homes**” (Edwards). Management emphasized a **pivot toward regional/local/custom builders** (better pricing/less commoditized), which also signals that **public/entry-level channels are softer**: “**A lot of it was mix… regional, local, custom builder was better… than… production builders**” (Miller).

**Direction vs last 4 quarters:**  
- FY25 1Q–2Q: demand/deliveries **deteriorating** (job volumes down; affordability headwinds).  
- FY25 3Q: volumes still down, but **backlog and mix quality improving** (see below).

### Backlog Quality & Conversion / Timing
Backlog is cited as **improving**, but there’s a notable **conversion lag**—backlog growth not yet turning into revenue. That’s good for visibility but raises timing risk: “**Contract backlogs at key branches have grown year-over-year**” (Edwards) and “**It's not translated into revenue yet, but it's translated into backlog**” (Miller).

Multifamily is the key swing factor: same-branch multifamily sales fell “**down 7%**” (Edwards), and management is increasingly framing multifamily as a **timing/sequence issue** (cycle-time elongation, earlier-trade labor bottlenecks, project delays): “**There have been in certain select markets some project delays**” and “**If there is a significant inflection… you can start to have elongated cycle times on the multifamily side**” (Miller). Importantly, they **pushed the expected rebound later**: “**2026… probably… more weighted towards the back half of 2026 than the front half**” (Miller).

**Read-through:** backlog may be “better,” but **the install window is less predictable**, especially in multifamily, which can create quarter-to-quarter volatility.

## Margins & Incentives Mechanics (Be specific)
IBP is not a homebuilder, so “incentives/rate buydowns” are **second-order** (they matter through builder behavior, product mix, and cycle times rather than IBP directly funding buydowns). In FY25 3Q, margins were **stable/slightly up**, driven mostly by **mix and commercial contribution**, not cost deflation.

- Reported adjusted gross margin: “**Adjusted gross margin of 34%, an increase from 33.8% in the prior year period**” (Miller).
- Management explicitly attributed YoY margin lift to **customer/product/geographic mix**: “**…increase in margin… was in part related to a shift in customer, product and geographic mix**” (Miller).
- A key mechanical bridge disclosed in Q&A:
  - Mix headwind components (complementary products + distribution/manufacturing growth) were meaningful: “**Combined, that had about a 60 basis point headwind to gross margin**” (Miller).
  - Offset came from heavy commercial: “**…offset with 100 basis point… benefit… heavy commercial**” (Miller).
  - But that tailwind **does not compound next quarter**: “**We don't expect any incremental benefit coming from the heavy commercial business in the fourth quarter**” (Miller).
- Internal distribution continues to provide a measurable gross margin lever: “**Internal distribution… provided an almost 50 basis points benefit to gross margin**” (Miller).

**Incentives / rate buydown transmission:** management continues to frame affordability as the gating issue, implying builders are using more incentive tools to clear product, which tends to:
1) shift volume toward larger/production builders when they can subsidize financing (discussed earlier in FY24 3Q), and  
2) pressure IBP volumes in entry-level channels while IBP defends margin via **customer selectivity** and mix (regional/custom, Midwest/Northeast).

### Spec Mix / Product Mix Signals
IBP doesn’t report “spec mix” like builders, but it experiences the downstream effect via **entry-level vs custom, and production vs regional/local**. The current quarter’s narrative suggests:
- **Better relative performance in regional/local/custom** (higher job price) and/or **top-half geographies**: “**…overweight… to the top half of the country… Midwest and the Northeast… up low single digits**” (Miller).
- Entry-level softness remains the limiting factor: “**We would expect that to be the case until the entry-level inflects**” (Miller).

Product mix remains a margin swing: complementary products are growing and improving margin, but higher mix share can still dilute consolidated GM depending on relative margins and revenue mix: “**When the sales rate of the complementary products is higher… it is a negative to gross margin**” (Miller) even while “**Complementary products saw margin improvement of about 100 basis points**” (Miller).
