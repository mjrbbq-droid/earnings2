## HOV — Company Pulse (FY25 Q4, reported 2025-12-04)

### Demand, Sales Pace & Demand Quality (latest quarter anchored)
Demand reads as **interest/traffic improving, but conversion and actual net sales pace lagging**, suggesting affordability friction and/or a more incentive-driven close environment rather than organically tightening supply/demand.

- Management highlighted top-of-funnel improvement: **“Traffic increased significantly in three of the four months… buyer interest has grown compared to last year.”**
- But that did **not** translate into stronger monthly pace: **“Sales pace for each month in the fourth quarter was lower than the same months last year.”**

**Direction vs prior 1–3 quarters:** The “choppy”/hesitant buyer narrative persists (Q1–Q3), but Q4 is more explicit that **YoY monthly pace is down** even as traffic improves—i.e., **quality of demand/conversion is deteriorating**.

## Margins & Incentives Mechanics (be specific)
**Core margin pressure is incentive/buydown-driven, and the buydown “tax” is rising.** Q4 margins landed near guide, but the setup for Q1 implies further step-down as the company prioritizes pace and absorbs higher buydown costs.

**What happened in Q4**
- Adjusted gross margin: **“Adjusted gross margin came in at 16.3%… near the high end of our guidance.”**
- Incentives as % of ASP rose sharply: **“In the fourth quarter of this year, incentives accounted for 12.2% of the average sales price.”** (up vs Q3 11.6%, Q2 10.5%, Q1 9.7%)
- Incentives are predominantly financing: **“The majority of this cost was attributed to mortgage rate buy-downs…”**
- The buydown tool is structurally embedded in the sales model: **“For the past two years, about 70% of our buyers have used mortgage rate buy-downs.”**

**Mechanics: why incentives are sticking**
- QMIs enable buydowns (fast delivery windows), reinforcing the spec-heavy approach; prior calls explicitly tied buydowns to near-term delivery timelines (e.g., Q2: **“Mortgage rate buydowns are typically on homes that can deliver in 90 days…”**).
- Management is exploring cost-efficient structures (ARM buydowns): **“We… have success with buying down a seven-year ARM… save cost, which helps margins.”**
- A *land vintage mismatch* is a key near-term drag: newer deals are underwritten with today’s higher incentives, but near-term deliveries are from older vintages that were not. They quantify this: **“62% of our land was initially controlled in either 2024 or 2025… 87% of our deliveries… from 2023 or earlier.”**

**Q1 guide and credibility check**
- Q1 FY26 margin guide implies further compression: **“Adjusted gross margin is expected to be in the range of 13% to 14%.”**
- Management attributes the step-down directly to buydowns and pace-over-price: **“This is lower… because of increased costs of mortgage rate buy-downs and our focus on pace versus price.”**
- They also set an inflection narrative: **“We expect our gross margin percentage to bottom in the first quarter of fiscal 2026 and to gradually improve.”**
  - This “bottoming” claim hinges on mix shifting toward newer, correctly-underwritten land: **“mix shift to newer land will help our margins.”** The risk is timing: deliveries are still disproportionately older-vintage.
