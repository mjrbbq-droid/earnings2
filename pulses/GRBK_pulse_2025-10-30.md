## GRBK — Company Pulse (FY25 Q3, call date 2025-10-30)

### Demand / Sales Pace & Demand Quality (latest quarter focus)
Demand is **steady but increasingly “incentive-supported”**, with improving buyer follow-through but weaker pricing/backlog quality.

- Management repeatedly frames the quarter as **stable demand**, but in the same breath acknowledges a **challenging environment tied to affordability and jobs**: “**Demand remained steady during each of the months during the quarter**…” and “**We continue to see a challenging sales environment… impacted by affordability challenges and a weakening job market**.”  
- **Demand quality (as measured by cancellations) improved meaningfully**: “**Low cancellation rate of 6.7% in Q3… improvement from 9.9% in Q2**.” This is a real positive signal vs the prior quarter’s slippage (Q2 cancel rate 9.9%).
- The tradeoff: the company is leaning harder into concessioning to maintain absorption—“**Driving our sales volume required price concessions and other incentives**…”

**Direction vs last 4 quarters:** demand described as *stable* in Q2 and Q3 after *improving* in Q1; cancellations **improved in Q3** after worsening in Q2.

Pricing power is **deteriorating**, with incentives stepping up again and becoming the primary demand “lever,” especially tied to quick move-ins.

- Reported concession load continues to rise:
  - “**Discounts and incentives increased year-over-year… to 8.1% from 5%**.”  
  - Order-level incentives rose further: “**Incentives for net new orders… increasing to 8.9%**.”  
- Buydowns remain central, particularly to clear spec: “**Rate buydowns remained a necessary tool to drive traffic and sales, especially with our quick move-in homes**.”  
- Management suggests some tactical restraint (not chasing the most aggressive rates): “**We haven't chased them down that low [to 4%] to generate the sales**,” while still highlighting targeted buydown outcomes: “**Rate, just under 5%. These are buy-down targeted rates.**”

**Direction vs last 4 quarters:** incentives moved from ~6.7% (Q1 orders) to 7.7% (Q2) to 8.9% (Q3). This is a clear deterioration in pricing quality even as “demand” is described as steady.

**Direction vs last 4 quarters:** backlog was up sequentially in Q1 (“ending backlog value increased 29% sequentially”), but has since **deteriorated in Q2 and further in Q3** (down 21% YoY in Q2; down 20% YoY in Q3), consistent with the higher QMI/spec mix and heavier incentives.
