## CVCO (Cavco) — Company Pulse (FY26 Q2, call date 2025-10-31)

### Demand, Sales Pace & Backlog Quality (latest quarter focus)
Demand looks **stable but increasingly bifurcated by region**, with Cavco actively managing output to keep backlogs from slipping—especially in the Southeast. Management framed backlog as steady in absolute terms, but the *way* it was held steady matters: **production was pulled back to protect backlog**. They note, “**we remained at about five to seven weeks**” of backlog, and “**Unit backlog was up slightly quarter to quarter… the result of selectively pulling back on production**.” That implies demand is not pulling backlog higher on its own.

Directionally vs the last 4 quarters:
- **Backlog**: basically flat at ~5–8 weeks across the period (FY25 Q3 “**six to eight weeks**”; FY25 Q4 “**five to seven weeks**”; FY26 Q1 “**essentially flat sequential backlog**”; FY26 Q2 “**five to seven weeks**”). The *quality* signal deteriorates slightly in Q2 because stabilization is achieved via production throttling rather than order acceleration.
- **Orders**: Q2 wholesale orders “**down just slightly**,” while Q1 saw “**orders increased**” with record shipments, suggesting momentum cooled sequentially.
- **Regional demand quality**: Q2 calls out Southeast shipment slowdown and explicit production cuts: “**We did need to slow our Southeast production in Q2… extended downtime… and production rate reductions**,” though they add “**Backlogs in our plants that serve the Southeast stabilize and edge up over the last month**.” Northern regions are the offset (stronger industry shipments per management commentary).

### Competitive / Inventory Backdrop & Spec Mix
Cavco is signaling a **more balanced supply environment**, not a price-war environment. “**At the moment, the market seems in balance with manufacturer production**,” and “**No one’s… going to drop price… Pricing is holding across the country right now**.” That’s consistent with the product being affordability-led, but it also reads like management is defending price discipline amid investor concern.

Inventory is a watch item in the retail channel:
- Q2: “**Inventories increased from higher finished goods at company-owned retail stores.**”
- This is notable because Q1 also flagged finished goods up at retail (and raw materials up to support production). If retail finished goods are building while wholesale orders soften, the risk is *channel stuffing* or slower retail sell-through (management doesn’t explicitly concede that—just states the inventory fact).

Spec mix: not discussed in homebuilder “spec” terms (CVCO manufactures), but **channel mix is the key analog** (retail vs wholesale) and it moved materially.

### Pricing Power & Sales Quality
Pricing appears **stable-to-firm**, with Q2 wholesale pricing “**essentially flat**” and management asserting “**Pricing did hold up across the board, including in the Southeast**.” This is an improvement from FY25 Q3–Q4 where ASP pressure was tied to pricing decreases and mix (“**product pricing decreases**” and “**more single-wide in the mix**”).

However, Cavco’s **reported ASP strength in Q2 is largely mix-driven**, not pure price:
- “**The significant upward movement in reported ASP was primarily the result of a higher percentage… from retail.**”
So the quality of ASP upside is **less about underlying pricing power** and more about selling through higher-ASP channels.

### Margins & Incentives Mechanics (be specific)
**Headline:** Factory-built housing margin held steady, with commodity relief (lumber) offset by tariff costs and regional utilization management; consolidated margin benefited from insurance normalization.

- Factory-built housing gross margin in Q2: “**22.9%… flat with the prior year quarter**.”

**Incentives / rate buydowns:** Not a major feature of the narrative (unlike site-built builders). Cavco’s “incentive” analog is **price discipline + channel mix + financing availability**. They continue to reference chattel market work and secondary market execution rather than promotional pricing: “**It’s a complex process to get to an actual agreement… They’re showing a lot of interest**,” and “**still a ‘lot of work’**” (per the provided summary). The absence of explicit discounting commentary, combined with “pricing holding,” suggests they’re trying to avoid margin erosion via overt incentives—but it also limits transparency on retail-level deal economics.
