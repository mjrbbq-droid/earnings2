## PHM (PulteGroup) — Company Pulse (FY25 Q3 focus)

### Latest Quarter Snapshot (FY25 Q3, 10/21/2025)
PHM’s quarter reads as **top-of-funnel healthier but demand quality weaker**, forcing **higher incentive intensity to convert elevated spec inventory**, with **backlog and margins rolling over**. Management is leaning on faster cycle times and cost control, but the operating playbook is increasingly about **turning standing inventory** rather than pricing power.

*Evidence:* “**traffic…was higher than last year, although conversion rates fluctuated from week to week**.” (Marshall) paired with “**net new orders…6% lower**” (Ossowski) and “**cancellation rate…12%…up from 10%**” (Ossowski).

---

## Sales Pace & Demand Quality (direction vs last 1–4 calls)
- **Absorption is sliding and volatility is showing up in conversion.** Q3 absorption **2.2** vs **2.4** last year: “**Q3 absorption rate of 2.2…down from…2.4**.” (Marshall). This continues the “challenging/volatile” framing from Q2 (“**demand conditions being volatile**,” Marshall), but the *result* is worse: lower absorption and weaker order growth.
- **Demand quality is deteriorating (higher cancels) as incentives rise.** Cancels moved to **12%**: “**cancellation rate…12%…up from 10% last year**.” (Ossowski). Earlier quarters emphasized stability (“**cancellation rate…11%…consistent with Q1**,” Q2), so this is a negative inflection.
- **Management explicitly concedes weaker underlying demand vs 2024.** “**core demand in 2025 has been below what we experienced in 2024**.” (Ossowski) — a notable specificity increase versus prior “volatile” language.

**Net-net:** traffic/leads appear supportive, but **conversion + cancellation** imply **softer buyer commitment** and/or **more incentive-dependent demand**.

## Backlog & Fulfillment Quality
- **Backlog is down materially YoY** (units and dollars), tightening visibility and increasing reliance on spec conversion: “**backlog of 9,888 homes…$6.2 billion…last year…12,089…$7.7 billion**.” (Ossowski).
- With spec production high, backlog becomes a less comprehensive demand indicator—yet the *decline still matters* because it reduces the cushion for deliveries and pricing.

## Specs, Inventory & Mix
- **Spec exposure is high and getting “sticky.”** “**49% were spec homes**” in production (Ossowski) and “**specs are likely to remain closer to 50%…next several quarters…higher than…40% to 45%**.” (Ossowski). This is a deterioration versus Q1/Q2’s “improving” trajectory/targets (e.g., Q2 “**anticipate…within…40% to 45%**,” Ossowski).
- **Finished spec inventory is above internal comfort levels**, increasing the probability of incremental pricing/incentive pressure: “**right around 2,000 finished specs…about 2 per average community…we’d ideally like…maybe 1.2**.” (Marshall).
- Operationally, PHM is aligning starts with sales to manage risk, but that also signals reduced confidence in demand: “**Q3 starts roughly equal sales**” (from summary; consistent with inventory-control posture).

## Margins & Incentives Mechanics (be specific)
- **Gross margin is rolling over and guidance was cut to move specs.** Q3 gross margin **26.2%**: “**down 80 basis points**.” (Ossowski). Q4 guide reduced to **25.5%–26.0%**: “**we now expect fourth quarter gross margins…25.5% to 26.0%**.” (Ossowski).
- **Incentives are the primary margin headwind, and they are stepping higher.** “**Incentives…8.9%…compares with 7.0% last year**.” (Ossowski). Directionally: Q1 ~8.0% → Q2 8.7% → Q3 8.9%.
- **Incentive mix matters: mostly price/feature concessions, not financing.** Management says financing tools are not the main lever:  
  - “**The financing incentives are probably about a third of the total incentive package.**” (Ossowski)  
  - “**forward commitments or mortgage buy-downs…not that impactful.**” (Ossowski)  
  - And importantly, they frame incentives as **ASP reducers**: “**any of the incentives…are a reduction in your gross sales price…a reduction in your ASP.**” (Ossowski)  
  This suggests PHM is protecting rate-buydown spend somewhat, while using **design center/spec discounts** (2/3 of package per summary) to clear specs—more directly dilutive to revenue per home.
- **Cost relief is present but insufficient to offset incentive drag.** “**build costs…at $79 per square foot, were consistent with prior year**.” (Ossowski). Flat costs help, but the YoY incentive step-up (≈+190 bps) overwhelms.
