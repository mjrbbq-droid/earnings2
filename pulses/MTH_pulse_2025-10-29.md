# MTH (Meritage Homes) — Company Pulse (FY25 Q3 focus)

## Snapshot: Latest Quarter (FY25 Q3, reported 2025-10-29)
Meritage’s operating model (short sale-to-close, spec-heavy, high intra-quarter conversion) is still working mechanically—**cancellations stayed low and conversion stayed extremely high**—but the quarter exposed a **deteriorating demand/affordability tape that is forcing “far north” incentive intensity and compressing margins**. Management is choosing to protect land values and throttle starts/land rather than “clear” specs at any price, which supports longer-run asset quality but risks near-term volume/mix pressure if the market remains soft.

Demand softness is explicitly acknowledged and notably **not rescued by lower rates**: “**Market conditions were softer than initially expected in Q3… ongoing affordability concerns**” and “**Lower mortgage rates has not translated to a notable improvement in demand or reduction in the use of incentives thus far**.” (Hilton/Lord)

## Sales Pace & Demand Quality (direction vs prior 4 quarters)
**Q3 demand tone deteriorated vs Q1–Q2 (stable) and vs FY24 Q4 (stable):** management moved from “healthy/normal spring” framing in Q1 to a clear “softer than expected” in Q3. (Hilton)

**Quality signals remain a relative bright spot** because the model reduces fallout:
- **Cancellations improved/low**: “**Cancellation rate of 11%… remained lower than historical average reflecting the limited time between a sale and closing.**” (Lord)  
- **Conversion is unusually high (but that’s a double-edged sword for visibility/backlog)**: “**more than 60% of our orders in Q3 closing this quarter, generating a 211% backlog conversion rate.**” (Hilton)

**Pricing power is weakening:** orders ASP down on incentive load—“**ASPs on order… $389,000 was down 4%… due to increased use of incentives and discounts.**” (Lord)

**Backlog is shrinking (deteriorating) and is becoming less useful as a forward indicator** under the strategy: “**Ending backlog declined… from approximately 2,300 units… to 1,700 units.**” (Lord)

## Specs, Mix, and Backlog Quality
Meritage continues to lean on specs/move-in-ready, but the **completed spec mix is now above their own comfort target**, raising incentive/price-risk if demand weakens further:
- “**Our completed specs comprise 47%… slightly higher than our target…**” (Lord)  
- The company is managing the pipeline to a **4–6 month spec supply** and is actively throttling starts: “**We intentionally slowed our starts by 19% year-over-year… within our target range of four months to six months' supply specs.**” (Lord)

**Interpretation:** The spec strategy supports speed and cancellation control, but Q3 shows the **margin cost of carrying near-finished inventory in a softer tape** (more incentives needed to convert, plus mix skew toward specs).
