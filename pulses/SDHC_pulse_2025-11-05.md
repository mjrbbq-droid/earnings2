# SDHC — Company Pulse (FY25 Q3 focus; direction vs prior 4 quarters)

## Demand, Sales Pace & Demand Quality
**Latest quarter (FY25 Q3):** Traffic appears to be holding in, but **conversion quality is weakening**, and the company is leaning harder on affordability tools to keep units moving. Management frames the core issue as confidence rather than foot traffic: **“Overall, demand stayed soft... buyer psyche and consumer confidence are the main headwinds.”** This shows up in the funnel: **“We've got traffic... But the conversions is just a little bit tougher.”**

**Sales pace decelerated late-quarter**, suggesting worsening elasticity and/or increased competitive noise. They explicitly quantified the slowdown: **“Monthly sales per community went from 2.8 in August... 2.0... September... In October... 2.0.”** Importantly, management is not assuming a snap-back: their “key changes” note they **“assume no velocity improvement”** into Q4.

**Direction vs prior quarters (last 4):**
- **Conversion:** deteriorated again in Q3 after a Q2 period where management suggested buydowns helped conversion (**Q2:** “We utilized forward commitments… helped boost conversion rates.” → **Q3:** tougher conversion).
- **Sales pace:** trending weaker at the margin; Q1/Q2 had mid-2s to high-2s monthly pace, but Q3 exits at ~2.0 and stays there.
- **Demand characterization:** the company continues to separate “traffic” from “demand” (traffic “decent,” demand “soft”), consistent with an affordability/psychology constraint rather than pure lead generation.

## Competitive & Inventory Backdrop / Spec Mix
**Competition is spec-heavy and promotional.** Management directly ties discounting to spec inventory on the ground: **“Competition... there's a lot of specs on the ground. That's where a lot of the discounting is taking place.”** SDHC is being pulled into that posture: **“Environment... pushing us more to a little spec heavy…”**

**Spec mix is rising vs historical norms, and that’s changing backlog character.** In Q2 they acknowledged the structural shift away from presale: **“Historically...70-plus percent presale versus spec...We're probably closer to 50%, 60% right now.”** In Q3, they imply this continues (and is market-driven), and claim a benefit in conversion/turns: **“Environment... pushing us more to a little spec heavy... resulting backlog conversions are higher.”** The *trade* is visible in margin and incentive intensity (see next section).

## Backlog Quality & Visibility
Backlog is being disclosed with **lower embedded margin than current realized**, signaling forward margin compression and/or heavier incentive load on homes still to close:
- **Q3 backlog:** **“Backlog... 760 homes... expected gross margin of approximately 20%.”**
- **Q3 realized close margin:** **“Gross margins on homes closed averaged 21% for the quarter.”**
- **Q4 guide:** **“Gross margin is projected to be in the range of 18.5% to 19.5%.”**

**Direction vs prior quarters (last 4):**
- Backlog units are down sequentially from Q2 (**858**) to Q3 (**760**), while expected backlog margin stepped down (Q2 **~21.5%** → Q3 **~20%**). This is consistent with an increasingly incentive-supported backlog and/or more expensive lot basis flowing through.

## Margins & Incentives Mechanics (be specific)
**FY25 Q3 margin compression is incentive- and lot-cost-driven, with buydowns escalating sharply.**
- Reported gross margin fell to **21%**, down sharply YoY: **“Gross margin came in at 21%... compares to 26.5% in the prior year.”**
- Management attributes the YoY decline to **lot costs + incentives/promotions**: **“Lower year-over-year margin reflects... higher average lot costs... and... rising incentives and promotional activity.”**

**Incentives are broad-based: closing cost support + price discounting + rate buydowns.**
- **Closing-cost incentives:** **“totaled approximately $9,500 per closing, up from $6,600.”**
- **Price discounts:** **“Pricing discounts were 1.8% of revenue, up from 1.2% last year.”**
- **Forward-commitment/rate buydown costs:** Q3 shows a major step-up and is accounted for as a revenue offset: **“Recognized $3.9 million in costs on forward commitments... as an offset to revenue.”** (vs Q2’s **$0.9m** and Q3 YoY **$0.185m** per the provided summary)

**Mechanically, the margin bridge looks like:**
1) **Higher lot cost basis** (structural) compressing gross margin.
2) **Higher incentives** (tactical) rising through the quarter: **“Incentives trended up through the quarter for sure.”**
3) **Rate buydowns** (financing subsidy) increasingly used to protect pace: **“We expect to continue to utilize these rate buydowns... to drive sales velocity.”**  
4) **Spec competition** pushing both price and financing giveaways (3.5% fixed on older specs): **“We introduced a really attractive 3.5% fixed rate on some older specs.”**

Net: The company is explicitly choosing to **buy conversion** in a spec-competitive environment, and the **Q4 margin guide (18.5%–19.5%)** implies the incentive/lot-cost headwind is not abating near-term.
