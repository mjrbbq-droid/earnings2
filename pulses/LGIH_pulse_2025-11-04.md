## LGIH — Company Pulse (FY25 Q3, reported 2025-11-04)

### Demand, Sales Pace & Backlog Quality (Latest Q Focus)
LGI’s order engine re-accelerated in Q3, and the *direction of change* is clearly better versus the softer Q2/Q4 period—helped by improved affordability optics as mortgage rates eased and the company leaned into targeted financing promos.

- **Sales pace inflected up materially:** “**Net orders coming in at 1,570 homes… 43.9% sequentially**” and backlog up “**61.5% sequentially**” (C. Merdian, prepared). This is a notable reversal from Q2’s “**muted demand environment throughout most of the second quarter**” (C. Merdian, prepared).
- **Backlog rebuilding is real, but conversion timing remains a watch item:** Management highlights longer contract-to-close as many entry-level buyers need more time to qualify/prepare: “**many of our buyers need additional time… This dynamic results in longer times between contract and close**” (E. Lipar, prepared). That language implies backlog quality is improving in *volume*, but not necessarily in *speed*.
- **Cancellations remain elevated and steady sequentially:** “**cancellation rate… 33.6%, similar to the prior quarter**” (C. Merdian, prepared). This is directionally worse vs Q1’s 16% range but consistent with Q2’s elevated level (“**32.7%**”, Q2).

### Competitive / Inventory Backdrop & Spec Mix
Q3 reads like an inventory-and-pace balancing quarter: stronger orders, but ongoing cleanup of older/aged units and a concerted effort to reduce WIP.

- **Inventory rebalancing is explicit:** “**focus on rebalancing inventory in select markets**” (C. Merdian, prepared). Homes under construction fell sharply: “**down 40.8% sequentially**” (C. Merdian, prepared).
- **Finished lot position is heavy (creates both flexibility and carry pressure):** “**13,000 finished vacant developed lots, which is a little heavier than we typically would like**” (C. Merdian, Q&A). That can support starts/pace when demand is there, but also suggests capital intensity and potential pricing pressure if absorption slips.
- **Spec/aged inventory is still being worked through via discounts:** “**price discounts of up to $50,000 on select older inventory**” (E. Lipar, prepared). This is directionally consistent with Q2 commentary about “**leaning into incentives, especially on the older aged inventory**” (E. Lipar, Q&A).

## Margins & Incentives Mechanics (Be Specific)
**Reported gross margin held in-range, but the mix and incentive toolkit is doing the heavy lifting—and the quality of margin is pressured by wholesale and lot costs.**

- **Q3 gross margin:** “**Gross margin came in at 21.5%… in line with the guidance range**” (E. Lipar, prepared).
- **YoY pressure drivers are mix- and land-cost-driven:** “**decline… higher lot costs… and a higher mix of wholesale closings**” (C. Merdian, prepared). Wholesale closings were **15.3%** of closings (up from **9.1%** YoY per key changes), a recurring lever also seen in Q1/Q2.
- **Financing incentives are positioned as controlled (but supplemented with targeted price cuts on older specs):**
  - Management emphasizes discipline: “**take a thoughtful approach to financing incentives**” (E. Lipar, prepared) and “**Going to extremes on buy downs… is something we're working hard to avoid**” (E. Lipar, prepared).
  - Yet Q3 includes meaningful affordability programs: “**forward rate buy down commitment**” (E. Lipar, prepared) and “**a 3.99% 5/1 ARM product… well received**” (E. Lipar, Q&A).
  - And **explicit discounting exists** where inventory is aged: “**discounts of up to $50,000 on select older inventory**” (E. Lipar, prepared).
- **Cost line items are mixed:**
  - Some build costs easing: “**Costs right now are slightly down, which is a little bit of a tailwind to margins**” (E. Lipar, Q&A).
  - But financing cost drag persists: “**expect capitalized interest to remain elevated due to higher borrowing costs**” (C. Merdian, prepared).
- **Structural cushion from low owned-land basis:** “**average finished lot cost of approximately $70,000… supports margin stability**” (C. Merdian, prepared). This underpins management’s confidence in holding margin bands while using incentives tactically.

**Bottom line:** Q3 margin stability is credible within guidance, but it’s being maintained amid (1) higher lot costs, (2) meaningful wholesale mix, and (3) selective price cuts/buydowns—offset by slightly lower build costs and favorable owned-lot basis.
