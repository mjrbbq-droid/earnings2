# MHO — Company Pulse (FY25 Q3 focus)

## Latest Quarter Snapshot (FY25 Q3, reported 2025-10-22)
Demand is **stable but choppy**, with **slower sales pace and higher incentive intensity**, while the business is increasingly **inventory/spec-led**. Margin pressure is now explicitly tied to **mortgage rate buydowns** plus discrete inventory charges, against a backdrop of **high and rising finished inventory**.

- **Sales pace/demand quality:** Pace slowed to **2.7 homes/community/month vs 3.2** YoY, reinforcing softer absorption rather than a traffic/conversion win. Management frames the backdrop as still difficult: *“housing demand and overall market conditions remain somewhat challenging.”*  
  Supporting quote: *“monthly sales pace averaged 2.7 homes per community compared to ... 3.2”* (Schottenstein).
- **Buyer quality / cancellation:** Credit/down-payment metrics remain strong and cancellations are contained.  
  Supporting quotes: *“average credit score of 745”* and *“average down payments of around 16%.”* (Schottenstein); *“our cancellation rate for the third quarter was 12%.”* (Creek).
- **Spec/inventory exposure:** Sales are heavily tilted to inventory: *“75% were inventory homes.”* (Creek). Completed spec inventory rose sharply YoY (*“776 completed… and 3,001 total inventory homes”* vs *“555 completed… and 2,375 total”* prior year quarter—Creek), raising carrying/impairment and pricing risk.
- **Regional tone:** Texas/Florida (esp. Austin and FL west coast) read as weaker; Midwest/Carolinas steadier.  
  Supporting quotes: *“Austin… has … cold considerably.”* (Schottenstein); *“our Midwest and Carolina business… held up a little better than Texas and Florida.”* (Creek); *“Orlando... is stronger than Tampa and Sarasota”* (Schottenstein).

**Direction of change vs prior quarters (last 4):**
- Sales pace **down vs Q2** (3.0 pace in Q2 → 2.7 in Q3: *“We were pleased to record… three homes per community”* in Q2 vs 2.7 in Q3).  
- Spec/inventory mix **stayed extremely high** (Q1 65% inv sales → Q2 73% → Q3 75%).

## Competitive / Inventory Backdrop
Inventory is the central operational/financial risk in this setup.

- Inventory is up materially YoY and sequentially, with completed units rising to **776**.  
  Supporting quote: *“We… had 776 completed inventory homes and 3,001 total inventory homes.”* (Creek).

Net: MHO is using its inventory to *sell*, but that same inventory is now pressuring *price/margins* and driving **inventory charges**.

## Margins & Incentives Mechanics (be specific)
**Gross margin fell to 23.9% (down 320 bps YoY)**, with two clear drivers:
1) **Mortgage rate buydowns (primary):**  
   - Supporting quotes: *“The cost of such buydowns are the primary reason for the decline in our gross margins.”* (Schottenstein); *“Absent… inventory charges… margins are down about 250 basis points… majority… due to mortgage rate buydowns.”* (Schottenstein).
   - Promo intensity: *“we're offering rates in the very high-4s”* (Schottenstein).  
   - Mechanically, buydowns are a direct margin reducer (builder-paid financing concession) and are most effective on near-term delivery inventory (as described in Q2/Q1 windows).
2) **Inventory charges / impairments (incremental):**  
   - Supporting quote: *“60 basis points of the decline due to $7.6 million of inventory charges.”* (Creek).

**Mix also matters, even if management frames buydowns as the headline:**
- With **75% of sales as inventory homes** (Creek), mix is structurally dilutive vs BTO. Prior calls quantify this: specs run **~100–200 bps** lower margin (Q1/Q4): *“there is a slightly lower margin on specs… probably today around 150 basis points to 200 basis points.”* (Schottenstein, Q1).

**Cost offsets exist but aren’t winning the fight:**
- Construction inputs are not inflating; that’s helpful but insufficient versus incentive drag: *“subcontractors and suppliers relatively flat to down”* (Schottenstein).  
- Land cost is an offsetting headwind: *“we do have higher land costs coming through than we did a year ago.”* (Creek).
