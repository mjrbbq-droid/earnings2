## 1) INVESTMENT REGIME
PHM is in a **late-cycle absorption defense with elevated spec exposure and rising incentive load**. Upside is driven by faster construction velocity converting to closings/cash plus an active-adult cohort that is holding up better than entry-level. Downside is constrained by low balance-sheet risk and an aggressive buyback bid, but earnings are capped near-term by the need to clear spec at higher concession levels.

## 2) WHAT CHANGED THIS QUARTER

## 3) DEMAND REALITY (sales vs interest)
Community traffic was up YoY, but purchase behavior softened: net new orders were 6,638 (down 6% YoY) with first-time-buyer orders down 14% YoY. Absorption ran at 2.2 homes/community/month (below last year’s 2.4), cancellations ticked up to 12% from 10%, and backlog moved lower to 9,888 homes from 12,089 a year ago—reducing forward revenue coverage even as engagement remains present.

## 4) INVENTORY & MIX (the margin lever)
Production ended at 15,096 homes, with 7,369 (49%) spec; management expects spec production to stay closer to ~50% versus the 40%–45% target range. Finished spec count was ~2,000 homes, equating to about ~2 per community versus an internal “optimized” level closer to ~1.2.

## 5) MARGINS: MECHANICS ONLY
Incentives increased to 8.9% of gross sales price (vs 7.0% last year and 8.7% in Q2), with heavier concession intensity cited in Texas and the West versus Florida/Southeast. The Q4 gross margin range of 25.5%–26.0% embeds incremental give aimed at driving spec conversion, implying further price realization pressure rather than a cost-driven decline.

## 6) COST & OPERATIONS (timing, not totals)

Tariff impact was framed as minimal for Q4 but potentially +~$1,500/home starting in 2026 (timing risk pushed out of the current year P&L).
