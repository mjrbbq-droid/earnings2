# COMPANY PULSE — Beazer Homes (BZH)  
**Anchor quarter: FY25 Q4 (11/13/2025). Directional context: prior 3 quarters of FY25 only.**

## Demand, Sales Pace & Mix Quality (Latest Quarter Focus)
FY25 Q4 reads like a **spec-led clearance market** rather than a to-be-built demand environment. Management is explicit that the core problem is **conversion/sales pace**, not merely traffic: *“conversion and sales paces remain well below historical norms, and aggressive incentives, and move-in-ready specs are still required to sell homes.”* (Merrill, FY25 Q4)

**Direction vs prior quarters (FY25 Q1–Q3):** deterioration has persisted, with Q4 confirming that the spec/incentive toolbox is still doing the heavy lifting. In Q1 they described the step-down in momentum (*“softened in November and became materially weaker in December”*), and by Q3 cited *“Affordability concerns and rising new and used home inventories impacted both traffic and sales conversion.”* (Merrill, FY25 Q1/Q3). Q4 doesn’t show a clean inflection—more an operational response to a still-challenged demand tape.

**Backlog quality:** Q4 acknowledges backlog is **down entering FY26** and also implicitly less useful as a signal given the spec skew: *“Backlog is less and less predictive in a more spec-oriented market.”* (Goldberg, FY25 Q4 Q&A). That’s both a near-term volume headwind and a visibility caveat.

## Competitive / Inventory Backdrop
Management frames the environment as **highly competitive with excess inventory**—not just for BZH but across builders: *“builders work through excess inventory.”* (Merrill, FY25 Q4). This aligns with prior FY25 commentary that rising inventories were hurting conversion (FY25 Q3) and that Texas/Florida inventory levels were triggering more aggressive incentives (FY25 Q1).

**Texas:** sequential pace improvement is mentioned, but tone remains cautious: *“our full year forecast for all three of our Texas markets is subdued.”* (Merrill, FY25 Q4 Q&A). Net: a tactical improvement, not a confidence reset.

## Margins & Incentives Mechanics (Be Specific)
**Reported gross margin stepped down sharply in FY25 Q4 to 17.2%** and management ties it directly to (1) spec mix and (2) incentives:  
- *“The combination of a higher percentage of specs and larger incentives resulted in a 17.2% gross margin.”* (Goldberg, FY25 Q4)  
- *“Given the competitive environment currently, the margins on the specs we sold and closed in the quarter were below our expectations.”* (Goldberg, FY25 Q4)

### How the machine is working (and why it’s painful right now)
1. **Spec mix is now the primary margin governor.** Management essentially concedes the economic hierarchy: *“We don't love being at 75/25 because we absolutely do make more money on to-be-builts.”* (Merrill, FY25 Q4 Q&A).  
2. **Incentives are targeted but heavy at the low end.** They call out where the give is concentrated: *“Our most aggressive incentives have occurred in our communities priced below $500,000.”* (Goldberg, FY25 Q4). That implies (a) first-time/entry buyer payment sensitivity, and (b) higher competitive overlap where builders use financing to move units.
3. **FY26 Q1 guide implies a further margin trough (~16%).** Management attributes this to the same mechanics—spec + incentive + low to-be-built share: *“Adjusted gross margin should be around 16%… driven by the higher level of incentive on specs and the very low share of to-be-built sales.”* (Goldberg, FY25 Q4).  
4. **Rate buydowns are not disappearing; they’re being treated as uncontrollable.** The language suggests they are planning around sustained incentive pressure rather than assuming relief: *“This isn't about assuming incentives are going to come down… control what we can control.”* (Goldberg, FY25 Q4 Q&A). This is a credible stance (not baking in macro help) but also confirms margin recovery is not demand-led yet.

### Cost-down offsets (timing mismatch)
They cite meaningful build-cost relief, but with lag: *“we rebid our material and labor costs, which has resulted in savings of about $10,000 per home so far.”* (Merrill, FY25 Q4). However: *“These savings should be fully realized in our closings by the fourth quarter.”* (Merrill, FY25 Q4) — implying **FY26 is the bridge year**, with near-term (FY26 Q1) still depressed by mix/incentives.

## Backlog, Specs, and Closing Quality
**Spec dependence is increasing into FY26 Q1** (management guide): *“We expect to sell approximately 900 homes, with specs representing up to 75% of the total.”* (Goldberg, FY25 Q4). That’s unusually high and has three implications:
- **Lower margin capture** (less design-center/upgrades, more price-to-move economics).
- **Less predictive backlog** (management explicitly says so).
- **Higher sensitivity to competitor inventory actions** because specs compete head-to-head on immediate availability and payment.

A partial offset is **community vintage mix**: *“The 48 opened since April 1 have generated margins more than 200 basis points above our reported margins.”* (Goldberg, FY25 Q4). If those become “>1/3 of closings,” mix can help—but it must outrun spec/incentive drag.

## Guidance Credibility & Visibility
**Credibility:** Management’s near-term margin guide appears internally consistent with the stated mechanics (spec-heavy + incentive-heavy = trough). They are not promising a quick rebound and explicitly refuse to underwrite incentive normalization (*“This isn't about assuming incentives are going to come down…”*). That improves credibility.

**But visibility is structurally weaker** because (a) backlog is down and (b) backlog is “less predictive” in spec mode. That combination makes volume/margin cadence harder to forecast even if the cost-down program is real.
